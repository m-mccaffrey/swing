Filming tips

  • Side view: camera at 90° to the pitch path, facing your chest (or your back). The pitcher should be off to the left or right of the frame.
  • Whole body in frame from head to feet for the entire swing, camera still (tripod or fence).
  • Frame rate: 60 fps or slow motion if your phone can. Nothing to set: the swing's own speed is measured.
  • Sharp hands: film in bright light (outdoors in daylight is best) or in slow-motion mode. Both freeze the hands; in dim light they smear into a blur and the pose model has to guess where they are.
  • One person in frame, 2–8 seconds around a single swing.

      How it works

      1. Pose estimation. MediaPipe Pose Landmarker runs in your browser on each frame. Its 33 landmarks are converted to the 25-keypoint OpenPose BODY_25 format, the same format as the pro database.
      2. Normalization. The swing is flipped so the pitcher is always to the right, joints are relabeled as front and back side (so left- and right-handed hitters compare directly), left/right label mix-ups are repaired, and everything is scaled to your torso length. Both hands are on the bat until after contact, so a hand the model lost or misplaced (hidden behind your body, blurred) is put back next to the other one; those points are drawn hollow.
      3. Stance match. Your stance frame is compared joint by joint with every pro stance. The closest one is your match.
      4. Swing clock. Each swing’s tempo is measured from its own hand motion, so slow motion, frame rate and how fast someone swings don’t matter. Tempo itself is not compared.
      5. Beats. Load, foot plant, contact, extension and finish are found by lining your movement up with the pro’s and refining each one from your own motion. You can move any beat by hand. Between two beats, the swings are stretched to match.
      6. Feedback. About 28 checks compare what a side camera can measure at matching beats: stance width, hand position, weight shift, stride, head movement, front-leg brace, spine tilt, hip and shoulder turn, contact point, extension and finish.

      Limits: a single side camera sees the swing in 2D, so rotation is estimated from how much the hips and shoulders narrow, and joint angles are apparent angles. The starter database holds clearly labeled synthetic placeholder swings. Real MLB swings can be added with the database builder or from OpenPose output (see the README).
