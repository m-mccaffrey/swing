Filming tips

  • Side view: camera at 90° to the pitch path, facing your chest (or your back). The pitcher should be off to the left or right of the frame.
  • Whole body in frame from head to feet for the entire swing, camera still (tripod or fence).
  • Frame rate: 60 fps or slow motion if your phone can. You can tell us the slow-motion factor in the next step so timing is right.
  • Good light, one person in frame, 2–8 seconds around a single swing.

    How it works

    1. Pose estimation. MediaPipe Pose Landmarker runs in your browser on each frame. Its 33 landmarks are converted to the 25-keypoint OpenPose BODY_25 format, the same format as the pro database.
    2. Normalization. The swing is flipped so the pitcher is always to the right, joints are relabeled as front and back side (so left- and right-handed hitters compare directly), left/right label mix-ups are repaired, and everything is scaled to your torso length.
    3. Stance match. Your stance frame is compared joint by joint with every pro stance. The closest one is your match.
    4. Swing alignment. Dynamic time warping lines up your movement from the stance onward with the pro’s, so load, foot plant, contact, extension and finish line up even if your timing differs. Then key events are refined from your own motion.
    5. Feedback. About 30 checks compare what a side camera can measure: stance width, hand position, weight shift, stride, head movement, front-leg brace, spine tilt, hip and shoulder turn, contact point, extension, finish and timing.

    Limits: a single side camera sees the swing in 2D, so rotation is estimated from how much the hips and shoulders narrow, and joint angles are apparent angles. The starter database holds clearly labeled synthetic placeholder swings. Real MLB swings can be added with the database builder or from OpenPose output (see the README).

    Runs entirely in your browser. Videos never leave your device.
