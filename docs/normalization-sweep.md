# Normalization sweep

Generated by `npm run sweep` (scripts/normalization-sweep.mjs) on 2026-10-02.

**Method.** The procedural 3D swing model renders *the same swing* as placeholder pro A under many recording conditions. Each recording then goes through the full user pipeline: automatic stance pick and pitcher side, canonicalization, alignment with the pro, phase transfer and all 28 feedback checks. It is compared against pro A as stored in the database. Because the swing is identical, a perfect normalization reports **every check as "matches"**, a swing score of 100 and phases at their true times. Columns:

- **Swing / Stance**: swing-match and stance-similarity scores (%).
- **Phase error**: the worst error over load, foot plant, contact, extension and finish, in real milliseconds, against ground truth.
- **Clock**: the swing clock (frames per swing-second, measured from the hand-speed burst) divided by the true real-time frame rate. 1.00 means the swing was timed exactly. The tempo rows read 1.25 and 0.80 on purpose: a swing that is slower or quicker overall is put on the same clock as the pro.
- **Flags**: checks that wrongly said "minor" or "work on". For sensitivity rows, the checks that reacted.
- **Worst**: the largest deviation in units of that check's tolerance (≥ 1 means flagged).

The same conditions run as tests in `tests/normalization.test.mjs` on every push.

## What the sweep found and what changed

Found in the first run, fixed in the same change:

1. **Child proportions lowered the swing score (83%) for an identical swing.** Swing scoring compared raw poses, so different limb proportions read as different positions. Now every pro frame is rescaled per bone to the hitter's proportions measured at the stance (`proportionScales` / `rescaleBones`). Bone directions and their foreshortening are kept, so rotation still shows. The on-video ghost uses the same fitting, which also stops its shoulders and hips from keeping a fixed width while they rotate. Now 99%.
2. **A tilted camera caused false flags** (1 flag at 3°, 4 at 8°: weight shift, spine tilt, balance). Canonicalization now levels the image using the ground line through both feet at the stance (big toes, else ankles), averaged over ±150 ms and only for tilts of 1–12°. Now 0 flags up to at least 8°. The same leveling is ported to the Python tool, and parity is tested.
3. **Slow motion with the speed left at "real time" broke everything:** wrong stance pick, phases about 290 ms off, 14 false flags. The first fix detected slow motion from hand speed and offered a one-click correction. It has since been replaced (item 6): nothing about the playback speed needs to be entered any more.
4. **Contact was detected 1–2 frames off under keypoint jitter,** and around contact the shoulders turn about 17° per frame, so contact checks false-flagged (10 flags over 8 seeds at 5 px). Contact is now the centroid of the hand-speed peak instead of the single fastest frame: 1 borderline flag over 8 seeds. This also fixed the 10°-off-axis camera case.
5. **Stance values came from a single frame.** The stance reference, the stance checks and stance matching now average ±60 ms of the (still) stance.

Changed later:

6. **Timing is no longer compared; each swing runs on its own clock.** Frame rate, slow-motion factor and frame skipping are unreliable on real clips (YouTube replays, phone slow motion, thinned frames), and an overall slower or quicker swing is not what the feedback is about. The swing clock is now measured from the swing itself: the hands' fast burst (speed above 25% of its peak) lasts a fixed 0.586 swing-seconds. That sets the frames per swing-second used for stance detection, smoothing, event windows and charts, so nothing has to be entered. The comparison then lines up the six beats (stance, load, foot plant, contact, extension, finish) and stretches time linearly between them, so positions are compared at the same point of the swing. The timing checks were removed. The clock is within about 3% across 24–240 fps and 4×/8×/16× slow motion, and tempo changes of ±20–25% produce no flags. Beats can be adjusted by hand in the app and in the Python tool's window when detection misses.
7. **Hands: the pose model's worst joints.** On real clips the far hand hides behind the body (low confidence, position guessed), both hands blur at launch, and sometimes one hand is confidently put on the bat or the other arm. A doubtful wrist used to be dropped and filled with a straight line, which cuts the corner of the hand path at its fastest, or left missing when the gap was long. Now both hands are kept together until shortly after the hands' peak speed (`repairHands`): a doubtful wrist is placed next to a believable one, at the hands' spacing interpolated from frames where both were seen; a confident wrist with an impossible forearm length, or one that jumped away while the other moved on smoothly, is not believed; two doubtful wrists that agree are kept. Hand speed, which locates contact, is now smoothed over 50 ms instead of 17 ms, so a briefly misplaced hand no longer looks like a burst of speed. The swing clock is measured on the repaired hands. Over 48 seeded variations of each failure (30 and 60 fps, either hand, three severities): far hand lost with wild guesses 12 → 0 runs with false flags; far hand hidden but well guessed 12 → 0; one hand confidently misplaced 44 → 8; both hands blurred 0 → 2 (one borderline flag each; before, blurred hands were dropped, so the contact hand checks were skipped rather than passed).
8. **Discontinuity is penalized.** Each keypoint track used to be smoothed with a light Gaussian after straight-line gap filling, so a confident one-frame jump (a hand snapped to the bat, a knee flipping) was averaged in rather than rejected. Now every track is fitted with a robust smoothing spline (`robustSpline`): it stays close to confident detections, barely follows doubtful ones, pays a penalty for sudden acceleration (cutoff 6 Hz in swing time, so the same at any frame rate) and ignores any detection more than 0.2 torso lengths off the fitted path. Gaps are bridged along the curve instead of a straight line. Over 48 seeded variations each: confident one-frame glitches 4 → 0 runs with false flags, one hand confidently misplaced 8 → 2, keypoint jitter (3 and 6 px, 16 runs) 1 → 0 false flags. Smoothing harder than this (5 Hz) or rejecting closer points (0.15) started moving the extension beat of a real-tempo swing, so the settings stop short of that.
9. **The swing clock on a real slow-motion clip.** The first real pro entry (a roughly 10x slow-motion broadcast replay thinned to 15 fps) showed what synthetic noise hadn't: the hands moved about 0.04 torso lengths per frame while the pose model's frame-to-frame hand noise was 0.1–0.3, so the hand burst was lost in noise and the clock came out at 7.3 frames per swing-second instead of about 160. Every time window was then about 20x too short, so the hand repair and smoothing hardly ran, and the pro would have been stretched about 8x when aligned. Now, when beats are known (every pro entry, and a user's swing once its beats are set by hand), the clock comes from the beat spacing whenever it disagrees with the hand clock by more than 1.5x (`pickSwingFps`), and the hand clock is never below 0.7x the analyzed frame rate (a clip is never faster than real time). On that clip, between load and contact: largest hand separation 1.05 → 0.42 torso lengths, forearm-length variation 65% → 12–25%; 58% of its hand points are now honestly marked as estimates.

## Known limits

- **Camera far off perpendicular (25°)** changes the 2D picture in ways a single camera can't undo. Many checks drift. Keep the camera within about 10° of perpendicular to the pitch path, where results are unaffected.
- **Tempo and rhythm are not judged.** Time is stretched to line up the six beats, so a swing that is slower overall, or slower between two beats, compares the same as the pro's. The feedback is about positions at each moment of the swing.
- **Wrong beats mean wrong comparisons.** The beats are detected automatically (load at the top of the leg kick or where the hands are furthest back, foot plant when the front foot lands, contact at the hand-speed peak). Check them on the results page and move any that are off.
- Joint angles and hip and shoulder turn are *apparent* values in the camera plane. They are consistent across recordings of the same view, which is what the comparison needs.
- These conditions are synthetic. Real pose-model behavior (occlusion, motion blur, a bat hiding the hands) is only approximated here by jitter, dropouts, label flicker and a systematic keypoint bias.
